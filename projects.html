Data Strategy 01
June 2026 — August 2026

Data Project Intern,
Gorter Family Career
Advancement Center

Lake Forest College, supervised by Katie Isler

What it is

A data internship at Lake Forest College's Gorter Family Career Advancement Center, running approximately 15 hours per week for academic credit through mid-August 2026. The role is supervised by Katie Isler and focuses on making the CAC's non-credit internship program data usable: clean, structured, and actionable for staff who need to track and report on student outcomes.

Why it mattered

Career centers accumulate significant student outcome data every semester, but that data often lives in spreadsheets that were never designed for analysis. Without a clean pipeline, staff cannot answer basic questions about program performance, student demographics, or internship trends. This project creates the infrastructure that makes those answers possible, and leaves it in a state that does not require a data scientist to maintain.

What I did

Cleaned and restructured non-credit internship program records, standardizing inconsistent entries and resolving data quality issues across multiple fields. Built an evaluation analysis report documenting findings and methodology in Quarto. Developed a data dashboard using Microsoft Power BI and Excel to give CAC staff a visual, interactive view of program metrics. Deliverables were produced across Word, Excel, R, Python, Quarto, and Jupyter to meet the CAC's reporting needs.

Recruiter signal

End-to-end data pipeline ownership in a real institutional setting: messy raw records in, clean reproducible dashboard out. Multi-tool fluency across R, Python, Excel, Power BI, and Quarto delivered inside a single internship. Work that lives beyond the internship and gets used by staff.

Research Web 02
March 2026 — Present

IGODO Research
Project

Prof. Ike Desmond Odugu, Lake Forest College

What it is

A faculty-led research initiative at Lake Forest College examining how colonial-era language policies shaped the structure and content of educational systems across Africa. The project synthesizes historical records, linguistic data, and policy documents to map long-term institutional effects on learning, access, and knowledge transmission in post-colonial contexts.

Why it mattered

The language of instruction in a school system is not a neutral choice. It encodes assumptions about whose knowledge counts, who has access to higher education, and how colonial hierarchies persist in modern institutions. Understanding these dynamics requires serious historical and linguistic analysis, not just general claims. This project contributes that analysis in a documented, publicly accessible form.

What I did

Served as Student Research Assistant under Prof. Odugu. Conducted literature review and source analysis on colonial language policy across multiple African regions. Supported data organization and research documentation. Independently designed, built, and deployed the project's full public-facing website: a six-page platform at igodo.org featuring researcher profiles, project dossiers, a news system, full-bleed image cards, hash-based tab navigation, and YouTube video integration. Resolved cross-browser JavaScript compatibility constraints throughout. Deployed and maintained on Vercel with custom DNS configuration.

Recruiter signal

Ability to operate inside a faculty research context with real accountability, synthesize qualitative material into organized documentation, and independently ship a production-grade web platform outside any job description or course requirement.

Policy Research 03
April 2026 — June 2026

Data Science Intern,
Therapy Innovations LLC

BreakingNewsABA.com — Editor: Michael Pavlovskyi

What it is

A data science internship at Therapy Innovations LLC producing policy analysis for BreakingNewsABA.com: a national trade publication covering the business, regulatory, and clinical dimensions of the applied behavior analysis industry. Articles go through professional editorial review before publication and reach a readership of ABA providers, executives, and policy stakeholders.

Why it mattered

ABA telehealth policy changed significantly in the years following the pandemic. CMS CPT code revisions, evolving state Medicaid reimbursement rules, and shifting telehealth parity standards created real uncertainty for providers trying to stay compliant. Practitioners needed clear, accurate analysis of what changed and what it meant operationally; that is what this internship produced.

What I did

Researched and wrote publication-ready policy articles covering ABA telehealth regulations, CMS CPT code changes, and state-level Medicaid policy shifts. For each article: identified the relevant regulatory change, pulled primary source documents (CMS guidance, state Medicaid bulletins, congressional records), synthesized the material into structured analysis, and translated it into accessible prose for a professional non-academic readership. All work was reviewed and approved by editorial staff before publication.

Recruiter signal

Professional-grade written communication under editorial standards, not a class rubric. Demonstrated ability to absorb a complex regulatory domain quickly and produce output that serves a real audience. Bylined published work available for review.

Statistical
Programming in R

MATH 250, Prof. Andrew Gard, Lake Forest College

What it is

An accelerated summer course at Lake Forest College focused on applied statistical programming in R. The course covers the full workflow from data import and cleaning through exploratory analysis, visualization, and reproducible reporting. Core libraries include ggplot2, dplyr, tidyr, and readr, with final outputs produced in Quarto for professional-grade reproducible documents.

Why it mattered

Theoretical knowledge of statistics is not the same as the ability to implement an analysis cleanly from a raw dataset. This course closes that gap: every topic is applied to real data, every deliverable is a document that runs from end to end, and the standard is reproducibility, not just correct answers. For a career in analytics or data science, that distinction is the whole thing.

What I did

Completed assignments applying ggplot2 for statistical visualization, dplyr and tidyr for data manipulation and reshaping, and Quarto for producing fully reproducible analytical reports. Applied these tools to real economic and social datasets, building the habit of treating analysis as a document others can read, run, and verify, not just a file that produces a number.

Recruiter signal

Demonstrated quantitative fluency in R with a reproducibility standard: analysis that documents its own assumptions, runs cleanly from raw data, and produces output a non-technical reader can follow. Directly applicable to analytics, business intelligence, and research roles.

Spring — Summer 2026

Applied Micro &
Macroeconomics

ECON 110, Prof. Kent Grote, Lake Forest College

What it is

An accelerated double-session course at Lake Forest College covering the full arc of economics, both microeconomic and macroeconomic theory, in a single intensive sequence. The course covers production possibility frontiers, supply and demand, elasticity, cost curves, market structures (perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly), GDP measurement, inflation, the AD/AS model, and fiscal and monetary policy.

Why it mattered

Economics is the foundation of how I frame analytical problems: resource allocation, incentive structures, market behavior, policy tradeoffs. This course built the formal theoretical base that makes my data science work more than technical execution. Knowing how markets work, how firms make decisions, and how policy transmits through an economy makes every analytical question sharper and more grounded.

What I did

Completed four exams and four graded homework assignments across the accelerated double-session format. Targeted a 92% or above throughout. Applied economic modeling frameworks to quantitative problem sets, building the habit of translating real-world scenarios into formal analytical structures and back again. The accelerated format required disciplined independent study with no room for passive learning.

Recruiter signal

Formal grounding in economic theory combined with quantitative problem-solving under an accelerated timeline. The combination of micro and macro in a single sequence demonstrates breadth; the course structure demonstrates discipline and self-direction.
